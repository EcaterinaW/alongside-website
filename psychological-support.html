PSYCHOLOGICAL SUPPORT

Emotional support for children and their families.

Childhood cancer can affect the emotional well-being of the entire family. These organizations offer counseling, support groups, educational resources, and tools that can help children, parents, caregivers, and siblings cope.

♡

COUNSELING · SUPPORT GROUPS

CancerCare

Provides free emotional and practical support from professional oncology social workers, including counseling, support groups, resource navigation, and programs for children and families.

FAMILY · CAREGIVER SUPPORT

Cancer Support Community

Offers professionally led support programs for people affected by cancer, including children, teens, parents, caregivers, and families. Services may include counseling, support groups, education, and navigation.

CHILDREN · FAMILY RESOURCES

American Childhood Cancer Organization

Provides free resources created for children with cancer and their families, including books, comfort materials, journals, and tools designed to help children process difficult emotions and experiences.

SIBLING SUPPORT

SuperSibs

A program from Alex's Lemonade Stand Foundation that provides age-appropriate emotional support and activities for children and teens who have a brother or sister diagnosed with cancer.

Your child's care team can also help

Pediatric cancer centers often have social workers, psychologists, child life specialists, counselors, or other professionals who can support children and families during treatment. Ask your child's healthcare team what psychosocial services are available.

SUPPORT FOR THE WHOLE FAMILY

Asking for emotional support can be an important part of caring for both your child and your family.
